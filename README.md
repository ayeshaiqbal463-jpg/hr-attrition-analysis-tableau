# Employee Attrition Analysis (Tableau)

**MSc Business Analytics, Queen's University Belfast | HR Analytics module**

## The problem
A company with 1,467 employees was losing staff at a rate of 19%. Leadership wanted to understand what was driving people to leave, and to move its reporting from Excel to Tableau for deeper, data-driven decisions.

## What I did
- **Cleaned the data** in Excel and Tableau, fixing quality issues in gender, age, date of birth and total working years.
- **Built interactive Tableau dashboards** to compare employees who left with those who stayed, by department, age, gender, education, salary, job role, job satisfaction and work-life balance.
- **Created Tableau calculated fields** to clean inconsistent values and to measure attrition and retention rates by segment.
- **Compared my findings with published research** on employee attrition.
- **Turned the findings into practical recommendations** for the business.

## Key findings
- **Sales had the highest attrition rate (30.6%)**, compared with 13.9% in R&D.
- **Employees in their late 20s and early 30s were the most likely to leave**, especially men.
- **Leavers earned around 25% less**, with an average monthly income of $5,154 compared with $6,825 for those who stayed.
- **Poor work-life balance was a strong warning sign.** Attrition reached 31% at the lowest work-life balance level.
- **Retention rose steadily with job satisfaction**, from 74% at the lowest level to 85% at the highest.

## Recommendations
- Prioritise targeted retention work in Sales.
- Introduce clear salary bands, market benchmarking and retention bonuses.
- Offer career development and mentoring for younger employees.
- Use pulse surveys to spot falling satisfaction early and act on it.
